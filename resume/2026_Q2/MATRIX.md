# 2026 Q2 Resume Variant Matrix

Six framings, each through the writer→reviewer pipeline. The revised (`_v2`) versions are the canonical drafts — the originals are kept for soundbite mining and to show what the reviewer round changed.

All variants share the same career skeleton, contact line, and education. The deltas live in the **summary line**, **bullets emphasized per role**, and the **skills section ordering**.

## V1 — Cut-and-Dry Graphic Designer
*File: `variants_revised/V1_graphic_designer_v2.md` (~680 words)*

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| **Best-fit titles** | Senior Graphic Designer, Brand Designer, Visual Designer, Editorial Designer, Production Designer |
| **Strongest pull** | "Inherited four conflicting visual languages across product, marketing, and community surfaces; unified them into a single reusable identity kit in under 90 days" (Silent Labs) |
| **What it sacrifices** | The strategic / fractional / AI-engineer story is buried. Three current builds barely surface. PETA team-scaling is a non-emphasis. |
| **Risk** | Too quiet on AI for AI-fluent shops; too craft-conservative for fractional senior roles. Lowest ceiling but highest floor — ATS-safe across the design vertical. |

Reads as a real designer, not a recent grad with Photoshop on a list. The Firefly-inside-Adobe framing is unusual and reads as "knows what AI is good for, doesn't worship it." |
| **Pair with** | V2 (web designer) for any role that mixes brand and product — strongest "craft-first" hybrid. |

| **What it sacrifices** | Specialist depth signal — by covering everything competently, doesn't dominate any one vertical. Most likely to get the "jack of all trades" read. |

| **Hiring-manager read** | This variant works for founder/CEO readers ("can you ship our brand and our site and our growth?") and fails for department-head readers ("I need a specialist"). Read for the right context this is the strongest pitch; read for the wrong one this is a no. |
| **Pair with** | V6 (AI integration) when the founder explicitly wants AI-fluent breadth. Don't pair with V1 — too much redundancy. |

## What I'd ship first (if I had to pick one)

If forced to pick **a single resume to start applying with today**: **V3 (social strategist v2)**.

Reason: it's the most polished, has the most-numerous and most-verifiable metrics, the PETA tenure does the heaviest lifting, and the "two cost basins" framing is genuinely novel. It also has the broadest target — the same file serves Senior Social Media Manager, Content Strategist, Growth Marketer, and Head of Content. Only weakness: leaves $0–10K/yr roles unreachable as overqualified, but that's a strength in this market, not a flaw.

If picking **two**: V3 + V5 — V3 for mid-level / IC growth roles, V5 for fractional / senior strategic. V6 stays in the bench for AI-integration-specific applications.

If picking **three**: add V2 for any web-product-design role that crosses your inbox.

V1 and V4 are good to have but aren't the strongest battery for an active campaign — V1 lacks differentiation, V4 risks the generalist read.
