### Speaking Technical For The Non-Technical

I think we really need to take the feedback from recruiters to heart, Re: if they don't know the lingo, they just move on because the market is tight and they have so many to go through. It was sort of a "this kind of is a bummer but it is a logistical reality right now" kind of post on LinkedIn and omg so many recruiters all chimed in saying the same thing. I think this is particularly substantial because how how fast tech changed like we forget how far ahead we are and how far behind most hiring is. I just think that accommodating their implied advice, to speak in more layman terms, isn't too difficult because there are so many other places to get technical. If they get to my resume on the right desk, then the portfolio. 

I thought of this, then opened a random final resume, and picked a random line, and it said "Architected the "Parallel Volley Funnel" agentic workflow in Claude Code with custom MCP servers" which, for not even trying to find an overly technical blurb, probably speaks loudly to the rest. I don't think even technical people will really get "parallel volley funnel" without like a full explanation of the workflow. And even "MCP", not that it should be spelled out, but that we can say the same thing in simpler terms without sounding dumb. Honestly it will probably just make us seem humble and smart because making technical work accessible is not just half this battle, but translating is a huge part of visual storytelling or social media or marketing jobs for many companies. 

In general, the takeaway is, we have to speak to the gatekeepers first and foremost. We have other spaces for speaking to the rest.
